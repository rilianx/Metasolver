We address the three-dimensional irregular strip packing problem: polyhedra, possibly non-convex, must be placed by translation in a container with a fixed rectangular base so that the height is minimized. We propose an overlap-minimization heuristic that works with the exact geometry of the pieces, represented as unions of convex parts. Its new element is an exact axis move. The overlap of two parts is measured by the penetration depth of their relative position in their no-fit polytope; along any axis this function is zero outside an interval and concave inside it, so the best position of a piece along an axis is found by evaluating a few breakpoints only. The rest of the method adapts a few known components from two-dimensional nesting: a greedy construction, a strategic oscillation of the container height with guided local search weights, a compaction of the feasible layouts, and restarts. On the benchmarks of polyhedral pieces, the method improves the best known heights of 11 of 13 instances within two minutes, reaches in 10 seconds the quality that the previous exact local search attains in 250 seconds (time normalized to our hardware), and matches 24 of the 27 known optima of small instances. An ablation study shows that the speed of the move, the guided local search weights and the effort spent in each separation are the decisive elements.
