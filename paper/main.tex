\title{A beam search for the air cargo palletization problem with weight distribution constraints}

\author{Ignacio Araya\corref{cor1}}
\ead{ignacio.araya@pucv.cl}
\cortext[cor1]{Corresponding author.}
\affiliation{organization={Escuela de Ingenier\'ia Inform\'atica, Pontificia Universidad Cat\'olica de Valpara\'iso},
	addressline={Avenida Brasil 2950},
	city={Valpara\'iso},
	country={Chile}}

\begin{abstract}
We address the palletization of air cargo into unit load devices (ULDs): a set of heterogeneous
boxes must be loaded into ULDs of minimum total volume, where the ULDs have bevelled corners to
fit the fuselage, a weight capacity and a floor edge for the cargo net locks, the boxes have
allowed orientations and may be fragile, every box must be supported, and the center of gravity
of each ULD must lie in a given area around its geometric center. We adapt a block-based beam
search guided by a greedy algorithm (BSG), originally designed for the container loading problem,
to this problem. The blocks are generated and placed so that the support and fragility
constraints are met, blocks that cross a bevelled side are shifted to the nearest feasible
position, and the center of gravity is taken into account in the value of the solutions, with a final
translation of the load. The ULDs are loaded sequentially, and the
total volume is then reduced by removing a ULD, inserting its boxes into the others and loading
the rest into at most two smaller ULDs. On the 300 benchmark instances of Paquay et al.\ and with
the same parameters in all the settings, the algorithm improves on the state of the art in both
volume and weight distribution. At a comparable running time, it uses 2.9--3.8\% less ULD volume
and at most 1\% of its ULDs violate the center of gravity constraint, against 7--11\% of the ULDs
of the previous method; with a few seconds per instance, it uses 7.1--8.2\% less ULD volume, with
at most 0.5\% of its ULDs out of range. The differences in volume are significant in all the
settings.
\end{abstract}
