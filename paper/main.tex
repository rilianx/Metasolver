\begin{abstract}
We address the palletization of air cargo into unit load devices (ULDs): a set of heterogeneous
boxes must be loaded into as few ULDs as possible, where the ULDs have bevelled corners to fit
the fuselage, a weight capacity and a floor edge for the cargo net locks, the boxes have allowed
orientations and may be fragile, every box must be supported, and the center of gravity of each
ULD must lie in a given area around its geometric center. We adapt a block-based beam search
guided by a greedy algorithm (BSG), originally designed for the container loading problem, to
this problem. The blocks are generated and placed so that the support and fragility constraints
are met, blocks that cross a bevelled side are shifted to the nearest feasible position, and the
center of gravity is taken into account in the evaluation of the blocks and of the solutions,
with a final translation of the load and a grouped reload of the ULDs whose center of gravity is
out of range. The ULDs are loaded sequentially, and the solution is improved by eliminating ULDs
and by inserting the unloaded boxes with ruin and recreate. On the 300 benchmark instances of
Paquay et al.\ and with the same parameters in all the settings, the algorithm uses 8--16\% fewer
ULDs than the state of the art when the number of ULDs is unlimited, being worse in at most one
instance; with one ULD of each type, it leaves fewer boxes unloaded with fewer ULDs. Almost none of its
ULDs violates the center of gravity constraint, against 7--11\% of the ULDs of the previous
method.

\medskip\noindent\textbf{Keywords:} packing; air transport; three-dimensional bin packing;
beam search; weight distribution
\end{abstract}
