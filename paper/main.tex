\title{A beam search for the air cargo palletization problem with weight distribution constraints}

\author{Ignacio Araya\corref{cor1}}
\ead{ignacio.araya@pucv.cl}
\cortext[cor1]{Corresponding author.}
\affiliation{organization={Escuela de Ingenier\'ia Inform\'atica, Pontificia Universidad Cat\'olica de Valpara\'iso},
	addressline={Avenida Brasil 2950},
	city={Valpara\'iso},
	country={Chile}}

\begin{abstract}
We address the palletization of air cargo into unit load devices (ULDs): heterogeneous boxes must
be loaded into ULDs of minimum total volume, subject to bevelled ULD contours, a floor edge,
weight capacities, fragile boxes, support constraints and limits on the center of gravity (CoG)
of each ULD. We propose a beam search that builds each ULD with blocks of boxes generated and
placed so that the support and fragility constraints hold, shifts the blocks along the bevelled
contours, and includes the CoG in the value that guides the search, followed by a translation of
the whole load. The ULDs are loaded sequentially, and their total volume is then reduced by
replacing each ULD with at most two smaller ones. On 300 benchmark instances derived from real
data, the algorithm improves on the state of the art in both volume and weight distribution: at
a comparable running time it uses 3--4\% less ULD volume, and 7--8\% less with a few seconds per
instance, while at most 1\% of its ULDs violate the CoG constraint, against 7--11\% for the
previous method. The experiments show that most of the savings come from the reduction phase,
which considers the whole solution, rather than from choosing smaller ULD types during the
construction, and that handling the CoG inside the search, together with the final translation,
satisfies the constraint in almost all the ULDs without giving up the savings in volume.
\end{abstract}
