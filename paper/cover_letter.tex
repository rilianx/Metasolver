\begin{letter}{Editors\\Computers \& Operations Research}

\opening{Dear Editors,}

Please find enclosed the manuscript \emph{``A beam search for the air cargo palletization problem
with weight distribution constraints''}, which I submit for consideration for publication in
Computers \& Operations Research, in the area \emph{Optimization -- Approximate Methods}.

The manuscript addresses the palletization of air cargo into unit load devices (ULDs), a
three-dimensional multiple bin size bin packing problem with non-cuboid bins, a floor edge,
weight capacities, fragile boxes, support constraints and center of gravity constraints, derived
from real data of a Boeing 777. It continues a line of work on block-based beam search for
container loading published in Computers \& Operations Research (Araya and Riff, 2014; Araya,
Guerrero and Nu\~nez, 2017).

The paper is not a direct application of that method: a container loading algorithm does not
handle the features of this problem, and the contribution lies in the components that make the
approach work for air cargo:
\begin{itemize}
\item blocks that are built and placed so that the support and fragility constraints hold, with
	the support criteria and the padding material of the problem;
\item the handling of non-cuboid ULDs, by shifting blocks along the bevelled contours, and of the
	floor edge and the substructure, as fixed obstacles with dormant free spaces;
\item the center of gravity inside the search, through a penalty in the value of the solutions,
	combined with a translation of the load within an interval of feasible translations;
\item a multi-ULD framework whose reduction phase replaces each ULD by at most two smaller ones.
\end{itemize}

The algorithm is evaluated on the 300 benchmark instances in the three settings of the current
state of the art (He{\ss}ler et al., European Journal of Operational Research, 2025), with
parameters tuned once on a separate training set, an instance-by-instance comparison at the same
hardware-normalized running time, and an ablation study. It improves on the state of the art in
both volume and weight distribution: at a comparable running time it uses 3--4\% less ULD volume,
and 7--8\% less with a few seconds per instance, while at most 1\% of its ULDs violate the center
of gravity constraint, against 7--11\% for the previous method. The experiments also show that
most of the savings come from the reduction phase rather than from the construction.

All the solutions are checked by an independent validator, and the code, the instances, the
solutions and the scripts that reproduce the tables and figures are publicly available at
\url{https://github.com/rilianx/bsg-uld}.

This manuscript is original, has not been published, and is not under consideration for
publication elsewhere. The author declares no competing interests.
