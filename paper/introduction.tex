\section{Introduction}
\label{sec:intro}

Before a cargo aircraft takes off, the individual shipments are consolidated onto air cargo
pallets and into containers, the so-called unit load devices (ULDs), which are then loaded into
the aircraft. The shape of a ULD depends on its position in the aircraft: lower-deck containers
have bevelled bottom corners and main-deck pallets and containers have bevelled top corners, so
that they fit the fuselage. Building the ULDs is a daily operational task in air freight
terminals, where it has to be solved for many flights in a short time, and every ULD saved is
space and weight that can be sold or saved in fuel. At the same time, a ULD plan is only usable if it respects the practical
constraints of air transport: the weight capacity of each ULD, the allowed orientations of the
boxes, fragile boxes on which nothing can be placed, the stability of the load, and the weight
distribution, since the center of gravity (CoG) of each ULD must lie in a given area around its
geometric center \citep{Paquay2016}. Building the ULDs is one of the subproblems of the air
cargo load planning problem \citep{Brandt2019}.

Assuming that the cargo consists of rectangular boxes, the problem is a three-dimensional
Multiple Bin Size Bin Packing Problem (MBSBPP) in the typology of \citet{Wascher2007}, with
non-cuboid bins and additional constraints. \citet{Paquay2016} proposed a mixed integer
programming formulation of the problem, which can only solve small instances, and a set of
instances derived from a real set of 562 boxes and the six ULD types of a Boeing 777.
\citet{Paquay2018a} and \citet{Paquay2018b} proposed MIP-based and two-phase constructive
heuristics for these instances. Recently, \citet{Hessler2025} extended the problem with
further practical features (a floor edge for the cargo net locks, on which no box can be loaded,
a substructure to raise the floor, and padding material to support the boxes) and proposed a
randomized greedy search (RGS) based on a fast extreme-point insertion heuristic. The RGS uses
30\% fewer ULDs than the heuristic of \citet{Paquay2018b} in a fraction of a second per
instance, and it is, to the best of our knowledge, the state of the art for these instances.

In this paper we show that a different algorithmic paradigm, borrowed from the single container
loading problem, yields considerably better ULD plans, both in the volume of the ULDs and in the
weight distribution: the RGS leaves 7--11\% of the ULDs with the CoG out of range, while our
algorithm satisfies the CoG constraint in almost all of them. The beam search guided by a greedy
(BSG) algorithm \citep{Araya2014,Araya2017} loads a container with \emph{blocks} of identical
or similar boxes placed in \emph{maximal free spaces}, and selects the blocks with a beam search
in which each node is evaluated by completing its partial solution with a greedy algorithm. It
is among the best algorithms for the classical container loading problem
\citep{Araya2017,Araya2020}, but the air cargo problem differs from it in several respects:
the ULDs are not cuboids, the support of the boxes and the fragile boxes restrict how boxes can
be stacked, the CoG of every ULD is constrained, several ULDs of different types must be loaded,
and all the boxes must be loaded into ULDs of minimum total volume. A direct application of BSG
does not handle these features: blocks built as in container loading may rest on fragile boxes
or be partly unsupported, blocks placed at the corners of the free spaces may cross the bevelled
sides, the CoG is not considered, and BSG loads a single container.

From BSG we keep the general scheme: blocks of boxes \citep{Fanslau2010} placed in maximal free
spaces \citep{Parreno2008}, the VCS function to evaluate them \citep{Araya2017}, and the beam
search with greedy completions \citep{Araya2014}. The contributions of this paper are the
components that make this scheme work for air cargo, and the experimental study:
\begin{itemize}
\item \emph{Blocks under support and fragility constraints.} The blocks are built so that every
	box inside a block is supported and no box rests on a fragile one, and a block is placed only
	if the boxes of its base satisfy the corner or area support criterion of the problem, with
	padding material \citep{Hessler2025}. Since the support inside a block is guaranteed by
	construction, only the boxes at the bottom of a block are checked during the search.
\item \emph{Non-cuboid ULDs, floor edge and substructure.} A block that crosses a bevelled side
	of the ULD is shifted along the length of the ULD to the nearest feasible position, which
	keeps it on the same base. The floor edge and the substructure are modelled as fixed
	obstacles, and the free spaces above the edge, which have no support until boxes have been
	loaded below them, are kept dormant instead of being discarded.
\item \emph{The CoG inside the search.} The value of every partial and complete loading is
	penalized by the excess of the CoG deviation over the allowed one, so that the beam search
	takes the weight distribution into account during the construction instead of in a
	post-processing step \citep{Davies1999,Paquay2018b}. The final load is translated as a whole
	to center its CoG, within an interval of feasible translations that is updated in constant
	time, and the free spaces are anchored at the origin of the ULD so that the load stays
	grouped and can be translated.
\item \emph{A multi-ULD framework with a volume reduction phase.} The ULDs are loaded
	sequentially, choosing the type of each one by comparing short loadings of the candidate
	types of the rule of \citet{Hessler2025}. The total volume is then reduced by removing a ULD,
	inserting its boxes into the other ULDs by ruin and recreate \citep{Schrimpf2000} and
	loading the remaining ones into at most two smaller ULDs, a move related to the elimination
	of bins in local search for bin packing \citep{Faroe2003} and to the repacking into smaller
	bins of \citet{Ortmann2010}. When the number of ULDs is limited, the boxes that must be
	loaded in a ULD are given priority inside BSG, and the construction is restarted with
	priority to the boxes left out.
\item \emph{An extensive experimental study.} We tune the parameters once with irace
	\citep{LopezIbanez2016} on a separate training set and compare the algorithm instance by
	instance with the RGS on the 300 instances of \citet{Paquay2018b} in the three settings of
	\citet{Hessler2025}, at the same hardware-normalized running time and with more time. At a
	comparable running time our algorithm uses 2.9--3.8\% less ULD volume, and 7.1--8.2\% less
	with a few seconds per instance, with significant differences in all the settings; at the
	same time, at most 1\% of its ULDs violate the CoG constraint, against 7--11\% of the ULDs of
	the RGS. An ablation study shows that most of the savings come from the reduction phase,
	which considers the whole solution, and that the penalty of the CoG excess and the final
	translation together keep the CoG in range. All the solutions are checked by an independent
	validator, and the code, the instances, the solutions and the scripts are publicly
	available.
\end{itemize}
