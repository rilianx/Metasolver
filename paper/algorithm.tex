\section{Algorithm}
\label{sec:algorithm}

The algorithm loads the ULDs one at a time. Each ULD is loaded by BSG, a beam search guided by a
greedy algorithm, which builds the load with blocks of boxes placed in maximal free spaces
(Section~\ref{sec:alg-bsg}). The ULD-specific features (the bevelled corners, the edge and the
substructure, and the support and fragility constraints) are handled in the generation and the
placement of the blocks (Section~\ref{sec:alg-uld}), and the CoG constraint in the value of the solutions and with a
translation of the load (Section~\ref{sec:alg-cog}). Section~\ref{sec:alg-load} puts these components
together to load one ULD, and Section~\ref{sec:alg-multi} describes the multi-ULD framework.

\subsection{Loading one ULD with BSG}
\label{sec:alg-bsg}

\paragraph{Blocks.}
A block is a cuboid arrangement of boxes that is placed as a unit. Before loading a ULD, a set
of blocks is generated from the boxes still available, as in \citet{Fanslau2010} and \citet{Araya2014}: the blocks of
one box are the boxes in their allowed orientations, and new blocks are obtained by joining two
existing blocks along the $x$, $y$ or $z$ axis, so that every block has a guillotine structure.
A joined block is kept only if its boxes are available, it fits in the bounding box of the ULD,
the boxes fill at least a proportion $f_{\min}$ of its volume, and it is different from the
blocks already generated (same dimensions and same boxes); the generation stops when
$N_{\max}$ blocks have been generated. We use $f_{\min} = 0.98$, as in BSG
\citep{Araya2014}, so that the blocks are almost solid, and $N_{\max} = 500$
(Section~\ref{sec:exp-setup}). Each block stores its weight and its CoG, computed recursively from the two
blocks that form it, which allows us to evaluate the CoG of a load without visiting its boxes.

\paragraph{Free spaces and actions.}
The free space of the ULD is represented by the set of maximal free spaces, the largest
cuboids that do not intersect the placed blocks \citep{Parreno2008}. When a block is placed, the
free spaces that intersect it are replaced by their maximal parts outside the block, and the
spaces too small for any remaining box are discarded. Unlike \citet{Araya2014}, where each
space is anchored at its nearest corner of the container and the load grows from all the corners,
every space is anchored at its corner closest to the origin of the ULD, and the next space is
the one closest to the origin (ties broken by the larger volume). The load thus grows from one
corner and stays grouped, which leaves room to translate it to center its CoG
(Section~\ref{sec:alg-cog}).
An action places a block that fits in the selected space at its anchor corner, which may be
modified as explained in Section~\ref{sec:alg-uld}; a block is a candidate only if its weight
does not exceed the remaining weight capacity of the ULD. A state of the
search is a partial loading of the ULD, with its free spaces and the remaining boxes.

\paragraph{Evaluation of the blocks.}
Each block $b$ that fits in the selected space is evaluated with the VCS function of
\citet{Araya2017}:
\begin{equation}
\label{eq:vcs}
\mathrm{VCS}(b) = V(b)\,\bigl(1 - \mathrm{loss}(b)\bigr)^{\beta}\,\mathrm{CS}_p(b)^{\alpha}\,
	n(b)^{-\gamma},
\end{equation}
where $V(b)$ is the volume of the boxes of $b$, $\mathrm{loss}(b)$ is the proportion of
the volume of the free space that cannot be filled with the remaining boxes after placing the
block (estimated with one-dimensional knapsack problems on each dimension of the space),
$\mathrm{CS}_p(b)$ is the proportion of the surface of the block in contact with the placed
blocks and the walls of the ULD, where faces at a distance of at most a proportion $p$ of the
block dimension are considered in contact, and $n(b)$ is the number of boxes of the block.

\paragraph{Greedy and beam search.}
The greedy algorithm repeatedly selects the next free space and places the best evaluated block
in it, until no block can be placed. The value of a complete loading is its volume utilization
penalized by the CoG excess (Section~\ref{sec:alg-cog}). BSG \citep{Araya2014} is a beam
search in which the children of each node are generated with the best evaluated blocks for the
selected space, and each child is evaluated by the value of its greedy completion. At each
level, the $w$ children with the best greedy values are kept, and a total of at most $m$
children are generated, so that each node of the beam is expanded with about $m/w$ blocks. The
best complete solution found by any greedy completion is returned (Figure~\ref{fig:bsg}). BSG is
run iteratively with
increasing effort until the time limit $t$ is reached \citep{Araya2017}: it starts with $w = 4$
and $m = 16$, and after each run $w$ and $m/w$ are multiplied by $\sqrt{2}$. The search also stops
as soon as a solution loads all the available boxes with the CoG in range, since no better
solution exists for that ULD. The implementation includes the optimizations of
\citet{Guesser2026} for BSG, such as cache-friendly data structures and the memoization of the
greedy completions, which speed up the search without changing its result.

\subsection{ULD constraints}
\label{sec:alg-uld}

\paragraph{Bevelled corners.}
Each cut of a ULD is a half-plane $a_j x + c_j z \le d_j$ in the $x$-$z$ plane, and the usable space
is the intersection of the bounding box $[0, L] \times [0, W] \times [0, H]$ with all the cuts. A
block $[x, x+\ell] \times [z, z+h]$ (in the $x$-$z$ plane) satisfies cut $j$ if and only if its
corner that maximizes $a_j x + c_j z$ does, that is, if
$a_j (x + \ell\,[a_j > 0]) + c_j (z + h\,[c_j > 0]) \le d_j$. A block placed at the anchor corner
of a space may cross one of the cuts. In that case, the block is shifted along the $x$ axis,
inside the space, to the closest position that satisfies all the cuts: for a fixed $z$, each
cut with $a_j > 0$ gives an upper bound and each cut with $a_j < 0$ a lower bound on $x$,
\begin{gather*}
x \le \Bigl\lfloor \frac{d_j - c_j (z + h\,[c_j > 0])}{a_j} \Bigr\rfloor - \ell \quad (a_j > 0),
\\
x \ge \Bigl\lceil \frac{d_j - c_j (z + h\,[c_j > 0])}{a_j} \Bigr\rceil \quad (a_j < 0),
\end{gather*}
and the block is moved to the point of the resulting interval, intersected with the extent of
the space, closest to the anchor; if the interval is empty, the block is not placed in the
space. Since the cuts are
orthogonal to the $x$-$z$ plane, shifting the block along $x$ keeps it on the same base, which
is necessary for its support. The free spaces are still cuboids, so a space next to a cut may
contain positions that are not usable; they are simply not used by any block.

\paragraph{Edge and substructure.}
The floor edge is handled as a fixed obstacle: four strips of width $e$ and height $\delta$ along
the border of the floor, which no box may overlap and which give no support, since no cargo can
rest on the net locks (\citet{Hessler2025} model them in the same way, with non-stackable dummy
items). The initial maximal
spaces of the ULD minus these strips are the space inside the edge,
$S_1 = [e, L-e] \times [e, W-e] \times [0, H]$, and the space above the edge,
$S_2 = [0, L] \times [0, W] \times [\delta, H]$ (Figure~\ref{fig:edge}). Similarly, the
substructure is a fixed obstacle that fills the floor inside the edge up to the height $\delta$
and supports the boxes, so that $S_2$ is the only initial space. The only change to the
maximal-space algorithm is due to $S_2$: its base has no support until boxes of height $\delta$
have been loaded in $S_1$, and a space whose blocks are all unsupported would otherwise be
discarded for good, since spaces are only reduced. Such a space is therefore kept aside
(\emph{dormant}) and reconsidered after the next block is placed. Each ULD with an edge is loaded
twice, on its floor and on the substructure, and the best of the two loadings is kept; the blocks
are generated for each of the two loadings, since the space in which they must fit is different.

\paragraph{Support and fragility.}
The support criterion of the problem (the four corners of the base, or the area criterion of
Section~\ref{sec:problem}) is checked box by box. Let $R$ be the base of a box placed at height
$z > 0$, and let the \emph{direct} faces be the top faces of the placed boxes at height $z$ and the
\emph{padding} faces those at a height in $[z - \hbar, z)$. The box is supported if each of the
four corners of $R$ lies on a direct face of a non-fragile box or, with the area criterion, if
it overlaps some direct face and $A_d + A_p \ge o\,|R|$, where $A_d$ and $A_p$ are the areas of
$R$ covered by direct and padding faces of non-fragile boxes and $o$ is the minimum supported
proportion ($o = 0.9$ in the adapted settings); the raised floor of the substructure is also a
direct face. The box is infeasible if $R$ overlaps a direct face of a fragile box, and the
padding faces of fragile boxes give no support. Inside a block, the support is guaranteed by
construction: two blocks are joined along $z$ only if the lower block has a complete top face
without fragile boxes and the upper block fits on it, so that every box of the upper block has
its whole base, and in particular its four corners, on non-fragile boxes. Therefore, when a
block is placed on the base of the selected space, only the boxes at its bottom are checked. Since the corner criterion allows gaps under a box, we also forbid placing a fragile box in
such a gap if it would touch the base of the box above. If no block can be placed in a space
because of the support, the space is discarded or, with an edge, kept dormant.

\subsection{Center of gravity}
\label{sec:alg-cog}

The CoG constraint is taken into account in two ways, besides the anchoring of the spaces at
the origin.

\paragraph{Translation of the load.}
Once a ULD has been loaded, the whole load can be translated horizontally without changing its
feasibility, as long as every block stays inside the ULD and its cuts and, if the ULD has an
edge, the blocks below the height $\delta$ stay inside the edge and the blocks on the
substructure stay on it. These conditions define an interval of feasible translations in $x$
and in $y$: a block $[x_1, x_2] \times [y_1, y_2] \times [z_1, z_2]$ allows
$\Delta x \in [-x_1, L - x_2]$ and $\Delta y \in [-y_1, W - y_2]$, intersected, for $\Delta x$, with
the bounds that the cuts give (as above, with $z$ fixed) and, if $z_1 < \delta$ or the block lies
on the substructure, with $[e - x_1, L - e - x_2]$ and $[e - y_1, W - e - y_2]$. The intervals of
the load are the intersections over its blocks, so they are updated in constant time when a
block is placed. The final load of each ULD is translated by
$\Delta x = \min\bigl(\max(L/2 - \bar x, \Delta x^{\min}), \Delta x^{\max}\bigr)$, and $\Delta y$ in the
same way, the feasible amount that brings its CoG $(\bar x, \bar y)$ closest to the center of
the ULD.

\paragraph{CoG excess.}
Let $(\bar x, \bar y, \bar z)$ be the CoG of a loading after the best feasible translation
$(\Delta x, \Delta y)$. Its CoG excess is
\begin{equation}
\label{eq:excess}
\varepsilon = \frac{\bigl(|\bar x + \Delta x - L/2| - d^x\bigr)^+}{d^x}
	+ \frac{\bigl(|\bar y + \Delta y - W/2| - d^y\bigr)^+}{d^y}
	+ \frac{(\bar z - d^z)^+}{d^z},
\end{equation}
where $(a)^+ = \max(a, 0)$. The CoG constraint is satisfied if and only if $\varepsilon = 0$.

\paragraph{Value of a solution.}
The value of a (partial or complete) loading with volume utilization $u$ is
\begin{equation}
\label{eq:value}
v = \frac{u}{1 + \lambda\,\varepsilon},
\end{equation}
where $\lambda \ge 0$ is a parameter. This value is used to compare the greedy completions in
BSG and to select the best loading of a ULD. A multiplicative penalty keeps the value of any
non-empty loading positive, so that an unbalanced loading is still preferred to an empty ULD.
With a large $\lambda$, the CoG constraint becomes almost a hard constraint; with
$\lambda = 0$, it is ignored.

The weight and the moments of the partial loading and its interval of feasible translations
are stored in each state, so that the CoG excess of a greedy completion is computed in constant
time per placed block.

\subsection{Loading a ULD}
\label{sec:alg-load}

The components of Sections~\ref{sec:alg-bsg}--\ref{sec:alg-cog} are combined in
\textsc{LoadULD} (Algorithm~\ref{alg:load}), which loads a ULD of type $k$ with the available boxes
$B$, with a time limit $\tau$ for BSG. Two optional arguments serve the multi-ULD phases of
Section~\ref{sec:alg-multi}: a partial loading $F$ of the ULD, whose boxes are kept at their
positions and completed with the boxes of $B$ (BSG starts from the state with the boxes of $F$
placed), and a set $M \subseteq B$ of boxes with priority (Section~\ref{sec:alg-multi}); both are
empty by default. If the ULD has an edge, two loadings are built, one on the floor and one on the substructure
(line~\ref{a1:for}). For each of them, the blocks that fit in that space are generated
(line~\ref{a1:gen}), BSG builds a loading within the time limit $\tau$ (line~\ref{a1:bsg}), the
loading is translated to center its CoG (line~\ref{a1:tr}), and the one with the larger value
$v$ (Equation~\ref{eq:value}) is kept (line~\ref{a1:best}). With a partial loading $F$, only
the configuration of $F$ (floor or substructure) is considered.

\begin{algorithm}[htbp]
\caption{\textsc{LoadULD}$(k, B, \tau, F = \emptyset, M = \emptyset)$: load a ULD of type $k$
with the available boxes $B$ and the time limit $\tau$ for each run of BSG, completing the partial
loading $F$ and giving priority to the boxes of $M \subseteq B$.}
\label{alg:load}
\begin{algorithmic}[1]
\State $S^* \gets$ empty loading
\For{$s \in \{\text{floor}, \text{substructure}\}$} \label{a1:for}
	\Comment{the substructure only with an edge; if $F \neq \emptyset$, only the $s$ of $F$}
	\State generate the blocks of $B$ that fit in a ULD of type $k$ loaded on $s$ \label{a1:gen}
	\State $S \gets \textsc{BSG}(k, s, B, \tau, F, M)$ \Comment{starting from $F$} \label{a1:bsg}
	\State translate $S$ to center its CoG \label{a1:tr}
	\If{$v(S) > v(S^*)$} $S^* \gets S$ \EndIf \label{a1:best}
\EndFor
\State \Return $S^*$
\end{algorithmic}
\end{algorithm}

\subsection{Loading several ULDs}
\label{sec:alg-multi}

In the pseudocode, a loading $S$ of a ULD is also identified with the set of boxes it contains, and
$\mathcal{U}$ denotes the set of loaded ULDs. Algorithm~\ref{alg:multi} outlines the multi-ULD framework. A sequential construction,
\textsc{Construct} (Algorithm~\ref{alg:construct}), loads one ULD at a time
(line~\ref{a2:cons}); when the number of ULDs is limited, the boxes left out are inserted into
the loaded ULDs and the construction may be restarted (lines~\ref{a2:ins}--\ref{a2:restart}); finally, a reduction phase lowers the total volume of the
ULDs, the objective of the problem (\textsc{Reduce}, Algorithm~\ref{alg:reduce}, line~\ref{a2:red}). The phases after the
first loading of each ULD load many ULDs again, so BSG is given a shorter time limit in them:
$0.1\,t$ in our experiments, both in \textsc{Insert} and in the reduction phase and when the
candidate types of a ULD are compared during the construction.

\begin{algorithm}[t]
\caption{Multi-ULD framework.}
\label{alg:multi}
\begin{algorithmic}[1]
\State $P \gets \emptyset$; $(\mathcal{U}^*, B^*) \gets (\emptyset, I)$ \Comment{boxes with priority, best solution}
\Repeat
	\State $(\mathcal{U}, B) \gets \textsc{Construct}(P)$ \Comment{loaded ULDs, unloaded boxes} \label{a2:cons}
	\If{$B \neq \emptyset$}
		\State $(\mathcal{U}, B) \gets \textsc{Insert}(\mathcal{U}, B)$ \Comment{unloaded boxes (limited ULDs)} \label{a2:ins}
	\EndIf
	\If{$V(B) < V(B^*)$} $(\mathcal{U}^*, B^*) \gets (\mathcal{U}, B)$ \EndIf \label{a2:keep}
	\State $P \gets P \cup B$ \label{a2:prio}
\Until{$B = \emptyset$ or three restarts} \label{a2:restart}
\State $\mathcal{U}^* \gets \textsc{Reduce}(\mathcal{U}^*)$ \Comment{reduction of the volume} \label{a2:red}
\State \Return $(\mathcal{U}^*, B^*)$
\end{algorithmic}
\end{algorithm}

\paragraph{Sequential construction.}
In \textsc{Construct} (Algorithm~\ref{alg:construct}), the type of the next ULD is selected as in
\citet{Hessler2025} (line~\ref{c:cand}): among the
ULD types into which the boxes that fit in the fewest available types fit, the type with the
largest total volume of the remaining boxes that fit in it is selected, ties being broken by the
larger ULD volume. A box fits in a ULD type if its weight does not exceed the capacity and, in
some allowed orientation, it can be placed alone on the floor inside the edge or on the
substructure. When this rule leaves several candidate types, each one is loaded with the time
limit $0.1\,t$ and the one that maximizes $L - \theta V$ is chosen (line~\ref{c:sel}), where $L$
is the loaded volume and $V$ the volume of the ULD, preferring the candidates with the CoG in
range; the chosen type is then loaded again with the time limit $t$ (line~\ref{c:load}). With $\theta = 0$ this is the type that loads the
most volume; a small $\theta > 0$ prefers a smaller ULD when it loads almost the same volume.
When the number of ULDs is limited, some boxes should be loaded in this ULD: those that fit in no
other available type, which would otherwise remain unloaded, and the boxes of $P$, which
the restarts give priority to (line~\ref{a2:prio} of Algorithm~\ref{alg:multi}). These boxes $M$
(line~\ref{c:prio}) are given priority inside BSG (line~\ref{c:load}): in the value $v$ of the
loadings and in the evaluation of the blocks, each box of $M$ counts as $1 + \omega$ times its
volume, with $\omega = 10$. The beam search thus favors the loadings that include the boxes of
$M$, and the greedy completions favor the blocks made of them. On the training instances with
limited ULDs, $\omega = 10$ left less volume unloaded than $\omega = 1$ and $\omega = 100$.

\begin{algorithm}[htbp]
\caption{\textsc{Construct}$(P)$: sequential construction; the boxes of $P$ are given priority
(restarts).}
\label{alg:construct}
\begin{algorithmic}[1]
\State $B \gets I$; $\mathcal{U} \gets \emptyset$ \Comment{remaining boxes, loaded ULDs}
\While{$B \neq \emptyset$ \textbf{and} some available ULD type fits a box of $B$}
	\State $K \gets$ candidate ULD types for $B$ \citep{Hessler2025} \label{c:cand}
	\If{$|K| > 1$}
		\State $k \gets \arg\max_{k' \in K} (L - \theta V)$ of $\textsc{LoadULD}(k', B, 0.1\,t)$ \label{c:sel}
	\Else{} $k \gets$ the type in $K$
	\EndIf
	\If{the ULDs are limited}
		\State $M \gets$ boxes of $B$ that fit in $k$ and are in $P$ or fit in no other available type \label{c:prio}
	\Else{} $M \gets \emptyset$
	\EndIf
	\State $S \gets \textsc{LoadULD}(k, B, t, \emptyset, M)$ \Comment{priority to the boxes of $M$} \label{c:load}
	\State $\mathcal{U} \gets \mathcal{U} \cup \{S\}$; $B \gets B \setminus S$
\EndWhile
\State \Return $(\mathcal{U}, B)$
\end{algorithmic}
\end{algorithm}

\paragraph{Insertion of boxes into the loaded ULDs.}
The phases after the construction rely on the same procedure, \textsc{Insert}
(Algorithm~\ref{alg:insert}), which inserts a pool $B$ of boxes into the loaded ULDs
$\mathcal{U}$, without adding ULDs, by ruin and recreate \citep{Schrimpf2000}. For
$\rho = 0.3$, $0.6$ and $0.9$, in this order (line~\ref{i:rho}), the loadings $S \in \mathcal{U}'$
of a copy of the solution are visited in decreasing order of free volume, and $B'$ is the current
pool. A set $R$ of boxes amounting to a proportion $\rho$ of the loaded volume of $S$ is removed,
taking the boxes with nothing on top of them from the highest one down, so that $S \setminus R$ is
still feasible ($\textsc{Ruin}(S, \rho)$, line~\ref{i:ruin}). Then
$S' = \textsc{LoadULD}(k_S, R \cup B', 0.1\,t, S \setminus R)$ completes $S \setminus R$ with the
boxes of $R \cup B'$ (line~\ref{i:load}). $S'$ replaces $S$ unless its CoG is out of range while
that of $S$ is not (line~\ref{i:cog}), and the boxes of $R \cup B'$ that $S'$ does not contain form
the new pool. The new solution $\mathcal{U}'$ is accepted if $V(B') < V(B)$
(line~\ref{i:acc}), and the procedure stops as soon as the pool is empty
(lines~\ref{i:stop} and~\ref{i:empty}).

\begin{algorithm}[htbp]
\caption{\textsc{Insert}$(\mathcal{U}, B)$: insert the boxes of the pool $B$ into the loaded ULDs
$\mathcal{U}$ by ruin and recreate; returns the new loaded ULDs and pool.}
\label{alg:insert}
\begin{algorithmic}[1]
\For{$\rho \in \{0.3, 0.6, 0.9\}$} \label{i:rho}
	\If{$B = \emptyset$} \textbf{break} \EndIf \label{i:empty}
	\State $\mathcal{U}' \gets \mathcal{U}$; $B' \gets B$
	\For{$S \in \mathcal{U}'$ in decreasing order of free volume}
		\State $R \gets \textsc{Ruin}(S, \rho)$ \Comment{top boxes, a proportion $\rho$ of the volume} \label{i:ruin}
		\State $S' \gets \textsc{LoadULD}(k_S, R \cup B', 0.1\,t, S \setminus R)$ \Comment{$k_S$: type of $S$} \label{i:load}
		\If{$S'$ is feasible \textbf{and} (the CoG of $S'$ is in range \textbf{or} that of $S$ is not)} \label{i:cog}
			\State $B' \gets (B' \cup R) \setminus S'$; replace $S$ by $S'$ in $\mathcal{U}'$
		\EndIf
		\If{$B' = \emptyset$} \textbf{break} \EndIf \label{i:stop}
	\EndFor
	\If{$V(B') < V(B)$} $\mathcal{U} \gets \mathcal{U}'$; $B \gets B'$ \EndIf \label{i:acc}
\EndFor
\State \Return $(\mathcal{U}, B)$
\end{algorithmic}
\end{algorithm}

\paragraph{Unloaded boxes and restarts.}
When the number of ULDs is limited and some boxes remain unloaded after the construction, they
are inserted into the loaded ULDs with \textsc{Insert} (line~\ref{a2:ins} of Algorithm~\ref{alg:multi}). If some are still
left out, the construction is restarted, and the boxes left out in the previous constructions
are given priority in the first ULD in which they fit (the set $P$ in
line~\ref{c:prio} of Algorithm~\ref{alg:construct}). This handles boxes that fit in several types but conflict with each other,
for instance two large boxes that fit only in the largest types and cannot share a ULD. The
solution with the smallest unloaded volume is kept (line~\ref{a2:keep} of Algorithm~\ref{alg:multi}), with at most three
restarts.

\paragraph{Reduction of the volume.}
The construction tends to use the largest ULDs, and its last ULDs are often far from full. The
reduction phase replaces ULDs by others of smaller total volume. The ULDs are considered in decreasing order of lost volume (the volume of the ULD minus
the volume of its boxes). The boxes of the selected ULD $S$ are freed and inserted into the other
ULDs with \textsc{Insert} (Algorithm~\ref{alg:reduce}, line~\ref{r:ins}). If all of them are inserted, $S$ is eliminated (line~\ref{r:elim}).
Otherwise, \textsc{NewULDs} (Algorithm~\ref{alg:newulds}, line~\ref{r:new}) loads the remaining
boxes $B'$ into one or two new ULDs, of types $k_1$ and $k_2$, whose total volume
$V(k_1) + V(k_2)$ is smaller than the volume of $S$; $k_2$ may be none, and $V(\text{none}) = 0$.
The pairs of types are tried in increasing order of total volume (line~\ref{n:for}): the ULD of
type $k_1$, the larger one, is loaded with $B'$ (line~\ref{n:load}), and the ULD of type $k_2$, if
any, with the boxes left over. The pairs without enough weight capacity, or in which some box
fits in neither type, are skipped without loading them. The first pair that loads all
the boxes with valid CoGs (in range, or out of range only if the CoG of $S$ was) replaces $S$
(line~\ref{n:ok}; line~\ref{r:repl} of Algorithm~\ref{alg:reduce}). Two new ULDs allow
replacing a large ULD by two small ones when the leftover boxes fit in no single smaller type
(Section~\ref{sec:exp-ablation}); we limit the move to two new ULDs to keep the number of
pairs, and of loadings, small.
Otherwise, the
solution $\mathcal{U}'$ returned by \textsc{Insert} is discarded and the next ULD is tried. After every change the
phase starts again, and it stops when no ULD can be changed. Every change reduces the total volume
of the ULDs, so the phase terminates; a change may increase the number of ULDs, when one ULD is
replaced by two smaller ones.

\begin{algorithm}[htbp]
\caption{\textsc{Reduce}$(\mathcal{U})$: reduction of the total volume of the loaded ULDs
$\mathcal{U}$.}
\label{alg:reduce}
\begin{algorithmic}[1]
\Repeat
	\For{$S \in \mathcal{U}$ in decreasing order of lost volume}
		\State $(\mathcal{U}', B') \gets \textsc{Insert}(\mathcal{U} \setminus \{S\}, S)$ \label{r:ins}
		\If{$B' = \emptyset$} $\mathcal{U} \gets \mathcal{U}'$; \textbf{break} \EndIf \label{r:elim}
		\State $\mathcal{N} \gets \textsc{NewULDs}(S, B')$ \label{r:new}
		\If{$\mathcal{N} \neq$ failure} $\mathcal{U} \gets \mathcal{U}' \cup \mathcal{N}$; \textbf{break} \EndIf \label{r:repl}
	\EndFor
\Until{no ULD was changed}
\State \Return $\mathcal{U}$
\end{algorithmic}
\end{algorithm}

\begin{algorithm}[htbp]
\caption{\textsc{NewULDs}$(S, B')$: load the boxes $B'$ left over from the ULD $S$ into one or two
new ULDs of smaller total volume. $\mathcal{K}_S$ is the set of pairs of available types $(k_1, k_2)$,
$k_2$ possibly none, with $V(k_1) \ge V(k_2)$ and $V(B') \le V(k_1) + V(k_2) < V(S)$, where $V(k)$ is the
volume of a ULD of type $k$ and $V(\text{none}) = 0$.}
\label{alg:newulds}
\begin{algorithmic}[1]
\For{$(k_1, k_2) \in \mathcal{K}_S$ in increasing order of $V(k_1) + V(k_2)$} \label{n:for}
	\State $S_1 \gets \textsc{LoadULD}(k_1, B', 0.1\,t)$; $R \gets B' \setminus S_1$; $\mathcal{N} \gets \{S_1\}$ \label{n:load}
	\If{$k_2 \neq$ none}
		\State $S_2 \gets \textsc{LoadULD}(k_2, R, 0.1\,t)$; $R \gets R \setminus S_2$; $\mathcal{N} \gets \{S_1, S_2\}$
	\EndIf
	\If{$R = \emptyset$ \textbf{and} the CoGs of $\mathcal{N}$ are valid} \Return $\mathcal{N}$ \EndIf \label{n:ok}
\EndFor
\State \Return failure
\end{algorithmic}
\end{algorithm}
