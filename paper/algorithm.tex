\section{Algorithm}
\label{sec:algorithm}

The algorithm loads the ULDs one at a time. Each ULD is loaded by BSG, a beam search guided by a
greedy algorithm, which builds the load with blocks of boxes placed in maximal free spaces
(Section~\ref{sec:alg-bsg}). The ULD-specific features (the bevelled corners, the edge and the
substructure, and the support and fragility constraints) are handled in the generation and the
placement of the blocks (Section~\ref{sec:alg-uld}), and the CoG constraint at several levels
of the search (Section~\ref{sec:alg-cog}). Section~\ref{sec:alg-multi} describes the
multi-ULD framework.

\subsection{Loading one ULD with BSG}
\label{sec:alg-bsg}

\paragraph{Blocks.}
A block is a cuboid arrangement of boxes that is placed as a unit. Before loading a ULD, a set
of blocks is generated from the boxes still available, as in \citet{Fanslau2010} and \citet{Araya2014}: the blocks of
one box are the boxes in their allowed orientations, and new blocks are obtained by joining two
existing blocks along the $x$, $y$ or $z$ axis, so that every block has a guillotine structure.
A joined block is kept only if its boxes are available, it fits in the bounding box of the ULD,
the boxes fill at least a proportion $f_{\min}$ of its volume, and it is different from the
blocks already generated (same dimensions and same boxes); the generation stops when
$N_{\max}$ blocks have been generated ($N_{\max}$ is tuned, Section~\ref{sec:exp-setup}). We use $f_{\min} = 0.98$, so that the blocks are
almost solid. Each block stores its weight and its CoG, computed recursively from the two
blocks that form it, which allows us to evaluate the CoG of a load without visiting its boxes.

\paragraph{Free spaces and actions.}
The free space of the ULD is represented by the set of maximal free spaces, the largest
cuboids that do not intersect the placed blocks \citep{Parreno2008}. When a block is placed, the
free spaces that intersect it are replaced by their maximal parts outside the block, and the
spaces too small for any remaining box are discarded. As in \citet{Araya2014}, each space is
anchored at its corner closest to a corner of the ULD, and the next space to fill is the one
whose anchor corner is closest (in Manhattan distance) to the corresponding corner of the ULD,
with ties broken by the larger volume. An action places a block that fits in this space at its
anchor corner, which may be modified as explained in Section~\ref{sec:alg-uld}. A state of the
search is a partial loading of the ULD, with its free spaces and the remaining boxes.

\paragraph{Evaluation of the blocks.}
The blocks that fit in the selected space are evaluated with the VCS function of
\citet{Araya2017}:
\begin{equation}
\label{eq:vcs}
\mathrm{VCS}(b) = V(b)\,\bigl(1 - \mathrm{loss}(b)\bigr)^{\beta}\,\mathrm{CS}_p(b)^{\alpha}\,
	n(b)^{-\gamma},
\end{equation}
where $V(b)$ is the volume of the boxes of the block, $\mathrm{loss}(b)$ is the proportion of
the volume of the free space that cannot be filled with the remaining boxes after placing the
block (estimated with one-dimensional knapsack problems on each dimension of the space),
$\mathrm{CS}_p(b)$ is the proportion of the surface of the block in contact with the placed
blocks and the walls of the ULD, where faces at a distance of at most a proportion $p$ of the
block dimension are considered in contact, and $n(b)$ is the number of boxes of the block. The
evaluation is penalized by the CoG deviation caused by the block, as explained in
Section~\ref{sec:alg-cog}.

\paragraph{Greedy and beam search.}
The greedy algorithm repeatedly selects the next free space and places the best evaluated block
in it, until no block can be placed. The value of a complete loading is its volume utilization
penalized by the CoG excess (Section~\ref{sec:alg-cog}). BSG \citep{Araya2014} is a beam
search in which the children of each node are generated with the best evaluated blocks for the
selected space, and each child is evaluated by the value of its greedy completion. At each
level, the $w$ children with the best greedy values are kept, and a total of at most $m$
children are generated, so that each node of the beam is expanded with about $m/w$ blocks. The
best complete solution found by any greedy completion is returned. BSG is run iteratively with
increasing effort until the time limit $t$ is reached \citep{Araya2017}: it starts with $w = 4$
and $m = 16$, and after each run $w$ and $m/w$ are multiplied by $\sqrt{2}$. The greedy
completions of successive runs share many states; we store the states visited by the complete
greedy runs (identified by a hash of their placed blocks), and when a greedy run reaches a known
state, the rest of the solution is rebuilt from memory instead of evaluating the blocks again.
This speeds up the search without changing its result. The implementation also follows the
optimizations proposed by \citet{Guesser}: the free spaces and the placed blocks are stored in
contiguous arrays, the candidate blocks are filtered by their dimensions before being evaluated,
the contact surface is computed only with the blocks near the selected space, the duplicate
blocks are detected with a hash during the generation, and the volume loss of a block is
computed only when an upper bound of its evaluation can enter the best ones. \todo{referencia
de la tesis de L. Guesser (titulo, universidad, ano).}

\subsection{ULD constraints}
\label{sec:alg-uld}

\paragraph{Bevelled corners.}
A block placed at the anchor corner of a space may cross one of the cuts of the ULD. In that
case, the block is shifted along the $x$ axis, inside the space, to the closest position that
satisfies all the cuts, which can be computed in closed form from the half-planes of the cuts;
if there is no such position, the block is not placed in the space. Since the cuts are
orthogonal to the $x$-$z$ plane, shifting the block along $x$ keeps it on the same base, which
is necessary for its support. The free spaces are still cuboids, so a space next to a cut may
contain positions that are not usable; they are simply not used by any block.

\paragraph{Edge and substructure.}
With a floor edge of width $e$ and offset $\delta$, the initial free spaces are the space above
the floor inside the edge and the space above the height $\delta$ over the whole floor. With a
substructure, the only initial space is the one above $\delta$, and the floor inside the edge
is raised to $\delta$. A space above the edge has no support until boxes have been loaded inside
it; instead of discarding it, it is kept aside (\emph{dormant}) and reconsidered after the next
block is placed. Each ULD is loaded twice, with and without the substructure, and the best of
the two loadings is kept.

\paragraph{Support and fragility.}
The support criterion of the problem (the four corners of the base, or the area criterion of
Section~\ref{sec:problem}) is checked box by box. Inside a block it is guaranteed by
construction: two blocks are joined along $z$ only if the lower block has a complete top face
without fragile boxes and the upper block fits on it, so that every box of the upper block has
its whole base, and in particular its four corners, on non-fragile boxes. Therefore, only the
boxes at the bottom of a block have to be checked when the block is placed. A block is placed on the base of the selected space, and it is feasible if
each box at its bottom is supported according to the criterion of the instance (the four corners
of its base or the area criterion of Section~\ref{sec:problem}), considering the top faces of
the placed boxes at the height of its base (direct support) or at most $\hbar$ below it
(support by padding material), and if it does not rest directly or through padding on a fragile
box. Since the corner criterion allows gaps under a box, we also forbid placing a fragile box in
such a gap if it would touch the base of the box above. If no block can be placed in a space
because of the support, the space is discarded or, with an edge, kept dormant.

\subsection{Center of gravity}
\label{sec:alg-cog}

The CoG constraint is taken into account in four ways.

\paragraph{Translation of the load.}
Once a ULD has been loaded, the whole load can be translated horizontally without changing its
feasibility, as long as every block stays inside the ULD and its cuts and, if the ULD has an
edge, the blocks below the height $\delta$ stay inside the edge and the blocks on the
substructure stay on it. These conditions define an interval of feasible translations in $x$
and in $y$, which can be updated incrementally when a block is placed. The final load of each
ULD is translated by the feasible amount that brings its CoG closest to the center of the ULD.

\paragraph{CoG excess.}
Let $(\bar x, \bar y, \bar z)$ be the CoG of a loading after the best feasible translation
$(\Delta x, \Delta y)$. Its CoG excess is
\begin{equation}
\label{eq:excess}
\varepsilon = \frac{\bigl(|\bar x + \Delta x - L/2| - d^x\bigr)^+}{d^x}
	+ \frac{\bigl(|\bar y + \Delta y - W/2| - d^y\bigr)^+}{d^y}
	+ \frac{(\bar z - d^z)^+}{d^z},
\end{equation}
where $(a)^+ = \max(a, 0)$. The CoG constraint is satisfied if and only if $\varepsilon = 0$.

\paragraph{Value of a solution.}
The value of a (partial or complete) loading with volume utilization $u$ is
\begin{equation}
\label{eq:value}
v = \frac{u}{1 + \lambda\,\varepsilon},
\end{equation}
where $\lambda \ge 0$ is a parameter. This value is used to compare the greedy completions in
BSG and to select the best loading of a ULD. A multiplicative penalty keeps the value of any
non-empty loading positive, so that an unbalanced loading is still preferred to an empty ULD.
With a large $\lambda$, the CoG constraint becomes almost a hard constraint; with
$\lambda = 0$, it is ignored.

\paragraph{Evaluation of the blocks.}
To guide the construction towards balanced loads, the VCS evaluation \eqref{eq:vcs} of a block
is divided by $1 + \mu\,\varepsilon'$, where $\varepsilon'$ is the CoG excess of the partial
loading after placing the block, including the best feasible translation, and $\mu \ge 0$ is a
parameter. The weight and the moments of the partial loading and its interval of feasible
translations are stored in each state, so that $\varepsilon'$ is computed in constant time for
each candidate block.

\paragraph{Grouped reload.}
Since the spaces are anchored at the corners of the ULD closest to them, the greedy and BSG tend
to fill the ULD from its walls towards its center. When the load does not fill the ULD, this
can leave it spread against several walls, with little room to translate it. Therefore, if the
CoG of the best loading is out of range, or the ULD is less than half full while boxes remain to
be loaded, the ULD is loaded again with all the spaces anchored at the corner closest to the
origin. The resulting load is grouped in one corner and can be translated to the center of the
ULD. The loading with the best value \eqref{eq:value} is kept.

Algorithm~\ref{alg:load} summarizes how a ULD is loaded.

\begin{algorithm}[htbp]
\caption{\textsc{LoadULD}$(k, B)$: load a ULD of type $k$ with the available boxes $B$.}
\label{alg:load}
\begin{algorithmic}[1]
\State $S^* \gets$ empty loading
\For{$s \in \{\text{without substructure}, \text{with substructure}\}$}
	\Comment{only if the ULD has an edge}
	\State generate the blocks for $B$ and a ULD of type $k$ with $s$
	\State $S \gets \textsc{BSG}(k, s, B, t)$, with spaces anchored at the closest corner
	\State translate $S$ to center its CoG
	\If{$\varepsilon(S) > 0$ \textbf{or} ($u(S) < 0.5$ \textbf{and} $S$ does not contain all of $B$)}
		\State $S' \gets \textsc{BSG}(k, s, B, t)$, with spaces anchored at the origin
		\State translate $S'$ to center its CoG
		\If{$v(S') > v(S)$} $S \gets S'$ \EndIf
	\EndIf
	\If{$v(S) > v(S^*)$} $S^* \gets S$ \EndIf
\EndFor
\State \Return $S^*$
\end{algorithmic}
\end{algorithm}

\subsection{Loading several ULDs}
\label{sec:alg-multi}

Algorithm~\ref{alg:multi} outlines the multi-ULD framework. It has a sequential construction
phase followed by two improvement phases, which share the same insertion procedure, and a final
replacement of the least utilized ULD by a smaller one.

\paragraph{Sequential construction.}
The ULDs are loaded one at a time with \textsc{LoadULD}, and the type of the next ULD is selected
as in \citet{Hessler2025}: among the ULD types into which the boxes that fit in the fewest
available types fit, the type with the largest total volume of the remaining boxes that fit in
it is selected (ties are broken by the larger ULD volume). A box fits in a ULD type if its
weight does not exceed the capacity and, in some allowed orientation, it can be placed alone on
the floor inside the edge or on the substructure. When the number of ULDs is limited, some boxes
may only fit in the selected type among the ULDs still available. If the loading of the ULD
leaves some of them out, the ULD is loaded again with these boxes first, and the load is then
completed with the other boxes, keeping the first ones at their positions.

\paragraph{Insertion of boxes into the loaded ULDs.}
Both improvement phases rely on the same procedure, \textsc{Insert}, which inserts a pool of
boxes into the ULDs already loaded, without adding ULDs. It applies four stages, each of which
visits the ULDs in decreasing order of free volume; the new loading of a ULD is accepted if it
loads more volume and its CoG is not out of range when the old one was not, and the boxes that
the ULD no longer loads return to the pool:
\begin{enumerate}
\item \emph{Reload from scratch}: the ULD is loaded again with \textsc{LoadULD} with its boxes
	and the boxes of the pool.
\item \emph{Reload with fixed boxes}: the boxes of the ULD are kept at their positions and the
	search continues with the boxes of the pool.
\item \emph{Reload with priority}: the ULD is first loaded only with the boxes of the pool that fit
	in its type, and the load is then completed with its own boxes and the rest of the pool. This
	stage gives a chance to large boxes that do not fit in the free space left by the current load.
\item \emph{Ruin and recreate} \citep{Schrimpf2000}: for $\rho \in \{0.3, 0.6, 0.9\}$, a proportion
	$\rho$ of the loaded volume of each ULD is removed, taking the boxes with nothing on top of them
	from the highest one down, so that the remaining load is still feasible; then the ULDs are
	completed with the removed boxes and the pool, keeping the remaining boxes fixed. The new
	solution is accepted if the volume of the pool decreases, and the stage is repeated while it
	decreases.
\end{enumerate}
The procedure stops as soon as the pool is empty.

\paragraph{Unloaded boxes and elimination of ULDs.}
When the number of ULDs is limited and some boxes remain unloaded after the construction,
\textsc{Insert} is applied to them. When all the boxes are loaded, the algorithm tries to eliminate
ULDs: the ULDs are considered in increasing order of utilization, the boxes of the selected ULD
are freed and inserted into the other ULDs with \textsc{Insert}, and if all of them are inserted,
the ULD is eliminated and the phase starts again; otherwise the next ULD is tried. The phase ends
when no ULD can be eliminated.

\paragraph{Smaller ULD.}
Finally, similarly to \citet{Hessler2025}, who reload the last ULD, the boxes of the least utilized ULD are loaded into a smaller
ULD type in which all of them fit, trying the types by increasing volume, and the change is
accepted if all the boxes are loaded and the CoG is not out of range when it was not before.

\begin{algorithm}[htbp]
\caption{Multi-ULD framework.}
\label{alg:multi}
\begin{algorithmic}[1]
\State $B \gets I$; $\mathcal{S} \gets \emptyset$ \Comment{remaining boxes, loaded ULDs}
\While{$B \neq \emptyset$ \textbf{and} some available ULD type fits a box of $B$}
	\State $k \gets$ next ULD type for $B$ \citep{Hessler2025}
	\State $S \gets \textsc{LoadULD}(k, B)$
	\If{the ULDs are limited and $S$ leaves out boxes that only fit in type $k$}
		\State load these boxes first and complete $S$ with the other boxes of $B$
	\EndIf
	\State $\mathcal{S} \gets \mathcal{S} \cup \{S\}$; remove the boxes of $S$ from $B$
\EndWhile
\If{$B \neq \emptyset$}
	\State $\textsc{Insert}(\mathcal{S}, B)$ \Comment{unloaded boxes (limited ULDs)}
\Else
	\Repeat \Comment{elimination of ULDs}
		\For{$S \in \mathcal{S}$ in increasing order of utilization}
			\State $B' \gets$ boxes of $S$; $\textsc{Insert}(\mathcal{S} \setminus \{S\}, B')$
			\If{$B' = \emptyset$} remove $S$ from $\mathcal{S}$; \textbf{break} \EndIf
		\EndFor
	\Until{no ULD was eliminated}
\EndIf
\State try to move the boxes of the least utilized ULD into a smaller ULD
\State \Return $\mathcal{S}$, $B$
\end{algorithmic}
\end{algorithm}
